Real radar physics, not just colours

Every orange pixel on the map comes from NASA NISAR radar, a published physical rule and a check against independent data. Here is how.

1 · Physics

Why L-band sees water under trees

NISAR sends a 24 cm radar wave. Water changes how it comes back.

Rough ground scatterssome of the wave returns.
Open water is darka smooth surface sends the wave away. HH and HV both drop.
Flooded forest is brightwater + trunk form a corner reflector. HH rises, HV stays.

The official flood map (Copernicus GFM) uses Sentinel-1 C-band. Under forest and in cities it marks the area "cannot see". That blank is where we look.

3 · Detect

Four rules, each one physics

Each pixel is compared with its own dry self, not with a fixed colour scale.

Open water

HH < −17 dB · HV < −24 dB

Mirror surface, dark in both channels. Thresholds tuned per site (below).

Flooded field

HH and HV drop ≥ 2.5 dB

Patch ≥ 0.5 km², ends ≤ −12 dB, HH drops at least as much as HV (water, not lost leaves).

Water under trees

HH up ≥ 3 dB · |ΔHV| < 1.5 dB

Double bounce, only within 3 km of detected water.

Not a flood

permanent water removed

Lake core, river, sea: always water, so not flood. Plus an optical ocean mask.

Optical teaches radar. On cloud-free days we pick the HH/HV thresholds that best match NASA's optical water map (OPERA DSWx-HLS), and score each day left out. Tonle Sap: −13.5 / −18.5 dB from 9 clear days.

4 · Compare

Only the water the official map cannot see

We count NISAR water only inside GFM's blank. Water both maps see is not counted as ours.

adds = NISAR water ∩ GFM "cannot see" ∩ both valid

area = pixels × 80 m × 80 m

more = adds ÷ GFM flood

GFM = Copernicus Global Flood Monitoring (Sentinel-1). Nearest map within ±2 days, put on our 80 m grid.

"More %" is shown only where GFM maps ≥ 5 km² of flood, so tiny bases don't inflate it.

7 · Limits

What we don't claim

Lower boundsShallow water in grass barely changes L-band. Read every number as "at least".
Candidates, not proofWater in the blank is a strong candidate. Flooded forest has no ground truth yet.
A sampleWettest pass per site, sites we picked. Not a global or average figure.
Provisional dataMost NISAR passes are still Provisional; calibration may change.
80 m gridGFM (20 m) and NISAR dates differ by ≤ 2 days. Small or fast floods blur.
Detector versionsSite numbers use detector v2. v3 (with coherence) is tested on one site so far.
